The problem

The western Rif burns almost every summer, and the warning comes too late

  • Recent large fires: Bouhachem 2022 (> 7,500 ha), Larache 2022 (> 10,500 ha), Derdara 2021 and Bouhachem 2020 (~1,100 ha each), two new fires in August 2025.
  • Drivers: hot, dry chergui winds, steep slopes, and villages (douars) inside the forest.
  • The gap: danger bulletins are province-scale, and satellite detections arrive after ignition. Nobody can say which 250 m cells need attention today.
  • Why satellites: NASA FIRMS gives the only consistent 20-year fire history of these remote mountains (MODIS since 2005, VIIRS 375 m since 2017).

Business use case

Who uses Wiqaya, and for which decision

End usersForest managers (national forestry agency ANEF), Civil Protection, provinces and communes. Residents of the villages inside and around the forest.
Their decisionEvery summer morning: where to send patrols, watch-tower crews and first-intervention teams, and whether today is dangerous enough to warn residents and restrict burning and works.
What they use todayProvince-scale danger bulletins, field knowledge, and fire detections (FIRMS, EFFIS) that come after ignition. No cell-level daily view; no alerts in Arabic and French.
What Wiqaya addsA 250 m risk map with alert zones and an explanation per cell, a daily weather danger index, a 20-year fire history calendar, SMS / email sign-up in Arabic, French and English, and an open GeoJSON API.
Business modelSubscription per protected area for institutions (dashboard, API, alert dispatch). Free citizen alerts, funded by the institutional contracts or by CSR partners. Built on open data, so there is no imagery cost.

Validation and limitations

A working, reproducible pipeline, not yet a reliable predictor

TestResult
Leave-one-year-out 2017–2024, logistic regression (selected)mean ROC-AUC per year 0.62 · global 0.65 · PR lift 1.64
Leave-one-year-out, random forestmean ROC-AUC per year 0.56 · global 0.60
Per held-out year2018: 0.99 · 2019: 0.88 · 2020: 0.80 · 2021: 0.68 · 2023: 0.23 · 2024: 0.06
Blind test, 2025 fires (39 cells)ROC-AUC 0.25 · 0 % of fire cells in the alert zones
  • Works on the large fire years (2018–2021).
  • Breaks on 2023–2025: the 2025 fires started in cells rated low risk.
  • Why: the strongest features (elevation, recent burns, aspect) describe terrain and history, not vegetation dryness or human access.
  • Few positives: only 345 fire cell-years, so the scores per year are unstable.
  • No leakage: fire history strictly before each date; 2025 never used for training or model selection.

Impact

Earlier, local, understandable fire information

  • SDG 13 Climate action (13.1): resilience to climate hazards in a region where heatwaves and chergui winds drive extreme fire seasons.
  • SDG 15 Life on land (15.1, 15.2): patrols and firebreak work targeted where the risk is highest.
  • SDG 11 Sustainable communities (11.5): early warning for the ~10,600 people living inside the forest.
  • Morocco: supports the national strategy Forêts du Maroc 2020-2030, ANEF and Civil Protection.
  • Arab region: built only on open data (FIRMS, Sentinel-2, Landsat, DEM, WorldPop, Open-Meteo). It can be replicated in the Mediterranean forests of Algeria, Tunisia, Lebanon and Syria.
  • Inclusive: Arabic-first, right-to-left interface, SMS for residents without smartphones.

Next steps for incubation

From proof of concept to an operational service

  1. Better predictors: add land cover, OpenStreetMap distances and daily Sentinel-2 NDVI / NDMI / NBR from the research notebook to the reproducible pipeline.
  2. Hyperspectral: EnMAP / Arab Satellite 813 maps of live fuel moisture and species.
  3. Better truth: validate on dNBR burned areas, not only FIRMS points.
  4. Operations: SMS gateway, a daily batch job, and an alert workflow with forest managers.
  5. Pilot: one fire season in Bouhachem, Derdara and Jbel Sougna, then other Mediterranean forests.
